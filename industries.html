Industries

Same technology.Different rules.

An AI assistant for a bank and one for a hospital might share most of their code. The rest (oversight, evidence, explainability) is what decides whether it can go live. We design for that part from day one.

Choose your sector

Banks · insurers · lenders · wealth managers · fintech

Faster decisions you can still explain.

Financial firms have huge volumes of documents and customer contact. AI can speed both up, as long as every outcome can be explained to the customer and the regulator.

KYC and onboarding documents

Extract and check data from IDs, bank statements and company filings, and flag anything that needs review.

Complaints triage

Classify complaints, spot signs of customer vulnerability and draft responses for handlers to approve.

Advice and call summaries

Summarise client meetings into structured file notes with the key disclosures highlighted.

Policy and procedure assistant

Give staff instant, cited answers from internal policies and regulatory handbooks.

What we design for: the FCA’s Consumer Duty and its focus on good customer outcomes, the PRA’s model risk management principles (SS1/23) for banks, UK GDPR rules on automated decisions, and clear audit trails.

NHS suppliers · private providers · life sciences · care

More time for patients, less for paperwork.

Admin takes up a large share of clinical time. AI can cut that burden, with clinicians always reviewing anything that affects care.

Clinical letter drafting

Draft discharge and referral letters from notes, for clinicians to review and sign.

Referral and inbox triage

Sort and route incoming correspondence so urgent items reach the right person faster.

Coding support

Suggest clinical codes from records to speed up coding and improve its consistency.

Research literature review

Search and summarise papers and trial data with citations to every source.

What we design for: MHRA rules on software and AI as a medical device, NHS DTAC requirements, DCB0129 and DCB0160 clinical safety standards, and special-category health data under UK GDPR.

Law firms · in-house legal · accountancy · consultancy

Reviews in hours, every claim cited.

Professional services sell expert judgement. AI should take on the reading and first drafts, while the professional stays accountable for the advice.

Contract review

Compare contracts against your playbook, flag departures and suggest fallback wording.

Due diligence

Work through data rooms to extract key terms, risks and change-of-control clauses into a report.

Knowledge management

Search precedents and past advice securely, respecting ethical walls and matter permissions.

Time and billing narratives

Turn activity records into clear, accurate time entries and bill narratives.

What we design for: client confidentiality and legal privilege, SRA expectations that firms supervise and stay accountable for AI-assisted work, and private deployment so client data is never used to train shared models.

Retailers · brands · marketplaces · D2C

Content, search and service that scale with peak demand.

Retail runs on thin margins and seasonal peaks. AI helps small teams handle catalogues and customer contact volumes that would otherwise need far more people.

Product content

Generate and check product titles, descriptions and attributes in your brand voice.

Smarter site search

Search that understands requests like “waterproof jacket for walking under £100”.

Customer service

Answer order and returns questions instantly, and hand complex cases to your team.

Demand forecasting

Forecasts by product and store that factor in promotions, seasonality and weather.

What we design for: UK consumer protection law, including the CMA’s direct enforcement powers under the DMCC Act 2024, honest AI-generated content, and clear disclosure when customers are talking to AI.

Manufacturers · warehouses · transport · utilities

AI that works where Wi-Fi doesn’t.

Physical operations need models that run on site, in real time and often offline. That’s where small custom models do their best work.

Visual quality inspection

Camera-based defect detection on the line, trained on images of your own products.

Predictive maintenance

Spot early signs of failure in sensor data, so repairs can be planned before a breakdown.

Engineering knowledge assistant

Give technicians instant answers from manuals, logs and past fixes, even offline.

Document automation

Process delivery notes, certificates and customs paperwork automatically.

What we design for: on-device and on-premises deployment, operational technology security, and a firm rule that AI assists people but never replaces safety-critical controls.

Local authorities · central government · housing · education

Better services that people can trust.

Public bodies face rising demand with tight budgets, and a higher bar for transparency. AI has to be explainable to the people it affects.

Casework support

Summarise case histories and draft correspondence so caseworkers spend more time with people.

Contact centre assistance

Help staff find the right answer quickly across complex policy and eligibility rules.

Consultation analysis

Group and summarise thousands of consultation responses, with traceable quotes.

Teaching and admin support

Lesson planning, feedback drafting and admin automation for schools and colleges.

What we design for: the Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard, the Public Sector Equality Duty, Department for Education guidance on generative AI in education, and accessible services for everyone.

In every sector

What every regulator wants to see.

The details differ, but UK regulators keep asking the same five questions about AI. We make sure you can answer them.

  1. Is it safe and does it work?

    Tested against a baseline with documented results, and monitored after launch.

  2. Can you explain it?

    Answers cite their sources, decisions have reasons, and people are told when AI is involved.

  3. Is it fair?

    Checked for bias across the groups it affects, with results recorded.

  4. Who is accountable?

    A named owner, clear human oversight and a process for challenging outcomes.

  5. Can people challenge it?

    A clear route to contest decisions and have a person review them, as UK data protection law requires.
